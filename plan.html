Shyam · Business plan · v1 · September 2026 · Confidential

A one-colour clothing house with an astronomical release calendar.

Direct only, no wholesale, no paid media. Twelve drops a year, each on a new moon, each sold out and never restocked. This document covers the market, the unit economics down to SKU level, the three-year model, operations, capital and the risks that actually matter.

What the business is

An all-black premium apparel label named after Krishna. Indian silhouettes cut with streetwear construction, priced ₹2,900–₹90,000, sold only on the night of the new moon and never restocked.

Eight to ten pieces per drop, 1,300–1,600 units. Sold direct from our own storefront to metro India and the diaspora. Nothing goes to wholesale, marketplaces or resellers.

Why it works

Scarcity we don't have to justify. The calendar is astronomical. Nobody — including us — decides when a new moon falls, so the release rhythm can never read as a marketing gimmick.

A category nobody occupies. Indian craft brands sell bridal at ₹80,000. Streetwear sells printed tees at ₹2,000. The ₹5,000–25,000 band with a genuine Indian reference is empty.

A moat that compounds. One colour done properly becomes a manufacturing capability, not a season's palette.

02Market

The gap is structural, not temporary.

India's premium apparel market is splitting in two directions and leaving the middle open. On one side, heritage couture priced for weddings. On the other, imported or imitation streetwear with no cultural content at all. The customer who wants both is currently buying Fear of God and explaining his own culture through a bandhgala at weddings.

Addressable buyers, India

Roughly 2.1 million urban men aged 24–34 in the top eight metros with declared spend above ₹1.5L a year on apparel. We need 0.9% of them buying twice a year to hit the Year 3 number.

The diaspora

London, Dubai, Toronto, the Bay Area. Higher basket, lower price sensitivity, and a stronger appetite for the reference. Expected at 30–35% of revenue by Year 2 with no local marketing spend.

Proof from adjacent markets

Aimé Leon Dore, Jacquemus and Ganni each built a house inside a decade by owning one specific world at ₹8k–40k, never competing with LVMH on price. That is the shape of this plan.

Market sizing is a planning estimate built from public census and consumption bands, not a commissioned study. Commission one before a priced round.

03Customer

He already owns a lot of black.

24–34. Metro India or the diaspora. Design, tech, film, food, architecture. Spends ₹8–20k on a single garment without deliberating. Owns Fear of God and Uniqlo in the same wardrobe.

He is quietly tired of the only two ways to look Indian: embroidery, or nothing. He does not want to be explained to, and he does not want to explain himself.

What we know about him

Buys on impulse within a window. Drop mechanics work because he is decisive and hates being sold to twice.

Values the make over the logo. Will read a gsm and a dye recipe. Will not read a brand story.

Resells. One-per-person creates a secondary market, which is proof of demand and free price discovery.

Recruits others. Acquisition is almost entirely word of mouth and the physical drop night.

What we deliberately don't do

No womenswear until Year 2. No footwear ever — the tooling cost and the returns rate break the model. No seasonal palette, in any year.

05The drop model

Twelve nights a year, decided by the moon.

One drop per amavasya, live at the astronomical instant, IST. One unit per piece per person. No restocks, no pre-orders, no back-in-stock notifications, no waitlist.

Unsold stock is not discounted. It is re-dyed as Raakh and released into the following year's archive at the same price. The brand never runs a sale, which protects both margin and the resale market.

Published rules, never broken

1. One drop per new moon.
2. One unit per piece, per person.
3. Nothing restocked, ever.
4. No discounts, at any time.
5. Drop 002 must be fully in hand before Drop 001 goes live.

The mechanic only becomes an asset once it is unbroken. A late drop costs the entire idea.

Why the margin is this high

No wholesale discount (typically −50%), no marketplace commission (−25%), no paid acquisition (−15–25% of revenue for most D2C), no markdowns. Removing those four is the entire delta.

Where it is fragile

The model assumes 85%+ sell-through. At 65% Year 1 EBITDA falls to ₹3.1 Cr; at 50% it falls to ₹1.4 Cr and the business still survives but stops self-funding growth.

Working capital

Inventory is paid before it sells, so each drop needs roughly ₹24 L in hand 60 days ahead. From Drop 004 the previous drop funds the next; before that it comes from the raise.

07Operations & supply chain

Black is a manufacturing problem before it is a design problem.

The whole brand rests on five repeatable dye recipes. Three dye houses are sampled in parallel and one is chosen on wash-test data, not on price. Every batch is logged against a reference swatch and rejected outside tolerance.

Cut-make-trim sits with two units in Tiruppur and one in Noida for the tailored pieces. No single vendor exceeds 40% of a drop.

The 60-day drop cycle

D−60 Fabric booked, dye lot approved against reference.
D−45 Cut and make begins; first-off inspected.
D−25 30 wash cycles on a pulled sample.
D−18 Finishing, labels, numbered cards.
D−10 Goods in, photographed, live-listed dark.
D−0 Site lights at the astronomical instant.

Fulfilment

Own 3PL in Bhiwandi for India, 48-hour dispatch. International consolidated weekly from Mumbai.

Returns

Exchange only, size for size, 7 days. No refunds — consistent with the one-per-person and no-restock rules.

The fade warranty

Free re-dye for life on Kajal. Modelled at 4% claim rate, ₹280 per claim. ₹1.7 L a year at Year 1 volume.

Stack

Shopify headless storefront, queue and bot mitigation from day one. A 1,310-unit drop with 20,000 watching is what scalpers automate.

08Go to market

No paid media, ever. It contradicts the product.

A brand built on withholding cannot buy attention without breaking its own premise. Acquisition is seeding, the physical drop night, and the resale market doing price discovery in public.

1 · Seed forty, not forty thousand

One unnumbered black tee, hand-delivered, no note, to forty specific people: DJs, chefs, two tattooists, stylists, diaspora accounts. No brief and no contract. If it needs a caption it isn't working.

2 · Open an empty room

The site goes live 60 days out as a black page with a countdown and nothing else. It should be genuinely confusing. The only shareable object is the URL.

3 · Take the number, not the email

SMS and WhatsApp only, one message per amavasya. In India that list is worth ten times an email list and we own it outright.

4 · One physical night

Drop 001 also opens for six hours in one unmarked room in Bandra, lit by a single lamp. Fifty units held back for whoever finds it. Every photograph out of that room is free media for a year.

5 · Let resale happen

Never fight it, never comment on it, always track it. The Drop 001 resale multiple is the single number that tells us whether this works.

6 · Give one away a year

On Janmashtami the Mor piece is not sold. 108 units, given to people who write in about the black they already own.

09Team & hiring

The first hire is not a designer.

It is a production and sourcing lead who has personally shipped garment-dyed black at 500-unit minimums. Black is a manufacturing problem before it is a design problem, and every failure mode in this plan runs through the dye house.

Headcount plan

Year 1 — 5: founder, production and sourcing lead, designer, ecommerce and ops, community and drop-night.
Year 2 — 11: add womenswear designer, QA, second ops, CX, finance, content.
Year 3 — 22: add store team, international ops, second production lead.

Fixed opex above is fully loaded at these headcounts.
