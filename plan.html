What the business is

An all-black premium apparel house named after Krishna. Indian silhouettes cut with streetwear construction, priced $38–$1,100, released only on the night of the new moon and never restocked.

Eight to ten pieces per drop, 1,300–1,600 units, sold direct from our own storefront. Duties paid at checkout, shipped worldwide. Nothing goes to wholesale, marketplaces or resellers.

Why it works

Scarcity nobody has to justify. The calendar is astronomical. No one decides when a new moon falls, so the rhythm can never read as a marketing device — and it opens simultaneously in every timezone.

A category nobody occupies. Indian craft brands sell occasion wear above $900. Global streetwear sells graphics with no cultural content. The $60–$300 band with a real Indian reference is empty worldwide.

A moat that compounds. One colour done properly becomes a manufacturing capability, not a season's palette.

02Market

The gap is structural, and it is global.

Premium apparel is splitting in two directions and leaving the middle open. On one side, heritage couture priced for weddings. On the other, streetwear with no cultural content at all. The customer who wants both currently buys Fear of God and has nowhere to express where he is from.

United States, United Kingdom, EU, UAE, Canada, Australia and India from Drop 001. One storefront, one currency, duties paid.

32 million people of Indian origin outside India, concentrated in exactly our launch markets, with higher basket and lower price sensitivity. This is the beachhead, not the whole market.

Then everyone else

The reference does not need to be understood to work. Rick Owens buyers do not read Nietzsche. Expect 40% non-diaspora by Year 2.

Proof from adjacent

Aimé Leon Dore, Jacquemus and Ganni each built a house inside a decade by owning one specific world at $100–400, never competing with LVMH on price.

Market sizing is a planning estimate from public consumption bands, not a commissioned study. Commission one before a priced round.

03Customer

He already owns a lot of black.

24–34. New York, London, Dubai, Toronto, Berlin, Mumbai, Bangalore. Design, tech, film, food, architecture. Spends $100–250 on a single garment without deliberating. Owns Fear of God and Uniqlo in the same wardrobe.

He does not want to be explained to, and he does not want to explain himself. That is the whole brief.

What we know about him

Buys on impulse inside a window. Drop mechanics work because he is decisive and hates being sold to twice.

Reads the make, not the story. Will read a gsm and a dye recipe. Will not read brand copy.

Resells. One-per-person creates a secondary market — proof of demand and free price discovery.

Recruits. Acquisition is word of mouth and the physical drop night, not advertising.

05The drop model

Twelve nights a year, decided by the moon.

One drop per amavasya, live at the astronomical instant in UTC, which means it opens at the same second in every market. One unit per piece per person. No restocks, pre-orders, back-in-stock notifications or waitlists.

Unsold stock is never discounted. It is re-dyed as Raakh and released into the following year's archive at the same price. The brand never runs a sale, which protects both margin and the resale market.

Published rules, never broken

1. One drop per new moon.
2. One unit per piece, per person.
3. Nothing restocked, ever.
4. No discounts, at any time.
5. Drop 002 fully in hand before Drop 001 opens.

The mechanic only becomes an asset once it is unbroken. A late drop costs the entire idea.

Why the margin is this high

No wholesale discount (typically −50%), no marketplace commission (−25%), no paid acquisition (−15–25% of revenue for most DTC), no markdowns. Removing those four is the entire delta. Manufacturing in India against USD pricing is the second.

Where it is fragile

The model assumes 85%+ sell-through. At 65%, Year 1 EBITDA falls to $390 K; at 50% it falls to $165 K — the business survives but stops self-funding growth.

Working capital

Inventory is paid before it sells: each drop needs about $30 K in hand 60 days ahead. From Drop 004 the previous drop funds the next; before that it comes from the raise.

FX exposure

Revenue in USD, costs in INR. A stronger rupee compresses margin; every 5% INR appreciation costs roughly 1.2 points of gross margin. Forward-cover from Year 2.

Duty & DDP

Duties paid at checkout so nothing is refused at the door. Modelled at $11 per order blended across the seven markets.

Payments

Stripe for global card and wallets, UPI for India. Gateway cost 2.6% blended, in variable opex.

Tax

Indian operating entity, export of goods. Structure and IP ownership to be confirmed with counsel before the raise.

07Operations & supply chain

Black is a manufacturing problem before it is a design problem.

The whole brand rests on five repeatable dye recipes. Three dye houses are sampled in parallel and one is chosen on wash-test data, not price. Every batch is logged against a reference swatch and rejected outside tolerance.

Cut-make-trim sits with two units in Tiruppur and one in Noida for tailored pieces. No single vendor exceeds 40% of a drop.

The 60-day drop cycle

D−60 Fabric booked, dye lot approved against reference.
D−45 Cut and make begins; first-off inspected.
D−25 30 wash cycles on a pulled sample.
D−18 Finishing, labels, numbered cards.
D−10 Goods in, photographed, listed dark.
D−0 Site lights at the astronomical instant, worldwide.

Fulfilment

3PL in Bhiwandi. India dispatch in 48 hours; international consolidated air twice weekly out of Mumbai, DDP.

Returns

Exchange only, size for size, 14 days internationally. No refunds — consistent with one-per-person and no restock.

The fade warranty

Free re-dye for life on Kajal, worldwide. Modelled at a 4% claim rate and $18 per claim including return freight.

Stack

Shopify headless, multi-currency, queue and bot mitigation from day one. A 1,310-unit global drop is exactly what scalpers automate.

08Go to market

No paid media, ever. It contradicts the product.

A brand built on withholding cannot buy attention without breaking its own premise. Acquisition is seeding, the physical drop night, and the resale market doing price discovery in public.

1 · Seed forty, not forty thousand

One unnumbered black tee, hand-delivered, no note, to forty specific people across New York, London, Dubai and Mumbai: DJs, chefs, two tattooists, stylists. No brief, no contract. If it needs a caption it isn't working.

2 · Open an empty room

The site goes live 60 days out as a black page with a countdown and nothing else. It should be genuinely confusing. The only shareable object is the URL.

3 · Take the number, not the email

SMS and WhatsApp worldwide, one message per amavasya. Higher intent than email everywhere, and we own the list outright.

4 · One physical night

Drop 001 also opens for six hours in one unmarked room in Mumbai, lit by a single lamp. Fifty units held back for whoever finds it. Every photograph out of that room is free media for a year.

5 · Let resale happen

Never fight it, never comment on it, always track it. The Drop 001 resale multiple is the single number that says whether this works.

6 · Give one away a year

On Janmashtami the Mor piece is not sold. 108 units, given to people who write in about the black they already own.

09Team & hiring

The first hire is not a designer.

It is a production and sourcing lead who has personally shipped garment-dyed black at 500-unit minimums. Black is a manufacturing problem before it is a design problem, and every failure mode in this plan runs through the dye house.

Headcount plan

Year 1 — 5: founder, production and sourcing lead, designer, ecommerce and ops, community and drop-night.
Year 2 — 11: add womenswear designer, QA, second ops, international CX, finance, content.
Year 3 — 22: add store team, international ops, second production lead.

Fixed opex above is fully loaded at these headcounts on an Indian cost base.
