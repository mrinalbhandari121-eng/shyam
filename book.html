This document combines the brand book and the business plan. The first half specifies the identity and how it is applied; the second half is the commercial case. Figures are indicative planning numbers, not audited accounts.

IThe idea

Western streetwear made black mean danger.
We are building it on the tradition where black means god.

Krishna is painted dark because the infinite has no colour. Shyam is his name and it is also the Sanskrit word for black. Every garment we make is black — not as edge, not as minimalism, but as devotion. Nobody has built that brand, and the houses that could afford to cannot credibly do it.

The name does the work

Shyam is a real Indian first name. Instantly legible to those who know it, completely opaque to everyone else — the exact split Supreme had in 1994.

One colour is a moat

Competitors dilute across a seasonal palette. We compound. In five years, black done properly is a manufacturing capability nobody copies in a season.

Devotion outlasts hype

Hype churns. Bhakti doesn't. The language comes from worship, not the street: the drop is a night, the customer is a follower.

IIThe mark

One disc, cut once at twelve.

It is amavasya — the moonless night the drops fall on. It is midnight, the hour he was born, in the dark half of the month. It is a hand on a dial standing at twelve, which is why the whole calendar system falls out of it.

Disc
True circle. Never an ellipse, never a stroke.
Slot width
5% of the diameter. At Ø20 mm that is 1 mm.
Slot depth
Exactly to the centre point. Not one per cent over.
Terminals
Flat and square, breaking the outer edge cleanly.
Clear space
One quarter of the diameter on all four sides.
Minimum
8 mm printed, 12 mm embroidered, 16 px on screen.
Under 20 px
Slot opens to 6.5% — the only exception in the system.
IIILockups & rules

Always

Cream on black is the default and covers nine tenths of everything. Gold is reserved for cast hardware and the annual Janmashtami piece. The mark may appear with no wordmark at all.

Never

  • Never outline it, stroke it, or leave it unfilled.
  • Never rotate the slot except on the drop calendar.
  • Never place the mark inside another frame or badge.
  • Never set the wordmark in gold.
  • Never add a shadow, gradient or bevel. It is one flat colour.
IVThe drop clock

The logo is the calendar.

The slot is a hand on a dial. Rotate it thirty degrees per drop and twelve positions cover a year — one for each amavasya. Drop 001 stands at twelve.

On the site it is the countdown. On a garment it says which drop the piece came from without printing a number. In a grid, the same twelve discs are the canvas repeat. One drawing, four jobs.

Drop dates are astronomical, not commercial. Nobody decides when a new moon falls, and it happens at the same instant in every market on earth.

VColour

We sell one colour, so we made five of it.

Each black is a named, repeatable dye-and-wash recipe. This is the part of the brand that becomes a manufacturing moat — and it is what the hangtag talks about instead of talking about us.

The fade warranty

Black greys after twelve washes and everyone knows it. Every garment ships with a numbered card: if Kajal fades within two years we re-dye it free, forever, anywhere in the world. Modelled at a 4% claim rate and $18 a claim.

The rule that matters

Devanagari is never letterspaced. Tracking breaks the conjuncts and the word stops being a word. Latin is tracked +180; Devanagari is set as drawn.

Equal, not subtitled

श्याम is part of the identity, not a translation of it. It appears at a size that reads as a signature, never as a caption.

Numerals

Drops, unit counts and prices are always tabular. A drop is 001, never 1. Editions are always out of 108.

Say less than you could.

The brand withholds. Copy is short, flat and specific — a dye recipe, a weight in gsm, a date. No adjectives doing work a fact could do. We never explain the reference; the people it is for already know.

How it sounds

Yes: “260 gsm compact cotton. Kajal, double-dipped. 300 made.”

Yes: “The room stays dark until the moon does.”

No: “Inspired by the timeless spirit of Lord Krishna, this luxurious piece…”

The line we sell against: You've been wearing black your whole life. You just didn't know whose it was.

What the business is

An all-black premium apparel house named after Krishna. Indian silhouettes cut with streetwear construction, priced $38–$1,100, released only on the night of the new moon and never restocked.

Eight to ten pieces per drop, 1,300–1,600 units, sold direct from our own storefront. Duties paid at checkout, shipped worldwide. Nothing to wholesale, marketplaces or resellers.

Why it works

Scarcity nobody has to justify. The calendar is astronomical, and it opens at the same second in every market.

A category nobody occupies. The $60–$300 band with a real Indian reference is empty worldwide.

A moat that compounds. One colour done properly becomes a manufacturing capability, not a season's palette.

XCustomer

He already owns a lot of black.

24–34. New York, London, Dubai, Toronto, Berlin, Mumbai, Bangalore. Design, tech, film, food, architecture. Spends $100–250 on a single garment without deliberating. Owns Fear of God and Uniqlo in the same wardrobe.

He does not want to be explained to, and he does not want to explain himself. That is the whole brief.

What we know about him

Buys on impulse inside a window. Drop mechanics work because he is decisive and hates being sold to twice.

Reads the make, not the story. Will read a gsm and a dye recipe. Will not read brand copy.

Resells. One-per-person creates a secondary market — proof of demand and free price discovery.

Recruits. Acquisition is word of mouth and the physical drop night, not advertising.

XIIThe drop model

Twelve nights a year, decided by the moon.

One drop per amavasya, live at the astronomical instant in UTC, so it opens at the same second in every market. One unit per piece per person. No restocks, pre-orders, back-in-stock notifications or waitlists.

Unsold stock is never discounted. It is re-dyed as Raakh and released into the following year's archive at the same price. The brand never runs a sale, which protects both margin and the resale market.

Published rules, never broken

1. One drop per new moon.
2. One unit per piece, per person.
3. Nothing restocked, ever.
4. No discounts, at any time.
5. Drop 002 fully in hand before Drop 001 opens.

The mechanic only becomes an asset once it is unbroken. A late drop costs the entire idea.

Why the margin is high

No wholesale discount (−50%), no marketplace commission (−25%), no paid acquisition (−15–25%), no markdowns. Removing those four is the delta.

Where it is fragile

Assumes 85%+ sell-through. At 65%, Y1 EBITDA falls to $390 K; at 50% to $165 K — survives, but stops self-funding.

Working capital

Each drop needs about $30 K in hand 60 days ahead. From Drop 004 the previous drop funds the next.

FX exposure

Revenue USD, costs INR. Every 5% rupee appreciation costs about 1.2 points of gross margin. Forward-cover from Y2.

XIVOperations & supply chain

Black is a manufacturing problem before it is a design problem.

The whole brand rests on five repeatable dye recipes. Three dye houses are sampled in parallel and one chosen on wash-test data, not price. Every batch is logged against a reference swatch and rejected outside tolerance. Cut-make-trim sits with two units in Tiruppur and one in Noida; no single vendor exceeds 40% of a drop.

The 60-day drop cycle

D−60 Fabric booked, dye lot approved against reference.
D−45 Cut and make begins; first-off inspected.
D−25 30 wash cycles on a pulled sample.
D−18 Finishing, labels, numbered cards.
D−10 Goods in, photographed, listed dark.
D−0 Site lights at the astronomical instant, worldwide.

Fulfilment

3PL in Bhiwandi. India in 48 hours; international consolidated air twice weekly out of Mumbai, DDP.

Returns

Exchange only, size for size, 14 days internationally. No refunds — consistent with one-per-person and no restock.

Fade warranty

Free re-dye for life on Kajal, worldwide. 4% claim rate, $18 a claim including return freight.

Stack

Shopify headless, multi-currency, queue and bot mitigation from day one.

XVGo to market

No paid media, ever. It contradicts the product.

1 · Seed forty, not forty thousand

One unnumbered black tee, hand-delivered, no note, to forty specific people across New York, London, Dubai and Mumbai. If it needs a caption it isn't working.

2 · Open an empty room

The site goes live 60 days out as a black page with a countdown and nothing else. The only shareable object is the URL.

3 · Take the number, not the email

SMS and WhatsApp worldwide, one message per amavasya. Higher intent than email everywhere, and we own the list.

4 · One physical night

Drop 001 also opens for six hours in one unmarked room in Mumbai, lit by a single lamp. Fifty units held back.

5 · Let resale happen

Never fight it, never comment on it, always track it. The resale multiple is the number that says whether this works.

6 · Give one away a year

On Janmashtami the Mor piece is not sold. 108 units, given to people who write in about the black they already own.
