Ninety-one marks, and the one that survived.

The full route from first sketch to resolved mark. Kept because the rejected work is the argument for the chosen one — every dead end here was a real constraint being discovered, not a change of taste.

Phase 05Flat reductionOne colour, two elements, no gradient

Stripped everything. No gold, no gradients, no frames — because gold on near-black has almost no value contrast and a framed medallion reads heritage-crest, not modern. A mark has to survive as a silhouette before it earns colour.

Phase 07The disc, cutTwelve subtractions from one form

Learned: every added element cost legibility below 20 px and gained nothing. The plain single cut — the version we started this phase with — outperformed all eleven developments of it.

Resolved

The disc, cut once at twelve.

Slot width 5% of the diameter, running exactly to the centre, flat ends, no radius. Under 20 px the slot opens to 6.5% — that single exception is the whole small-size specification.

It is amavasya, the moonless night the drops fall on. It is midnight, the hour he was born, in the dark half of the month. And because the slot is a hand on a dial, rotating it thirty degrees a drop turns the logo into the calendar.

It passes the tonal test, holds at 16 px, casts in brass, debosses on black board, and needs no wordmark to be recognised.
